# Spoken Testimony — HB26-1318 (Senate Transportation & Energy)

**Target length:** ~3 minutes (≈ 410 words at a natural delivery pace).
**Bracketed numbers** mark approximate elapsed time.

---

[0:00] Chair, members of the committee — thank you for hearing me today. My
name is Ashton Honnecke. I help organize the Brown Elementary Bike Bus in
[CITY], where every week a group of kids rides together, with adults, to and
from school by bicycle.

I'm here in strong support of HB26-1318, and I'm asking you to do the same
thing the House did: pass it.

[0:30] I want to focus on three things the bill fixes — fast, because they're
real.

**One: Colorado has never actually defined what a school zone is.** That
sounds like a technicality. It isn't. The doubled traffic penalties on the
books today depend on a definition we don't have. Police, courts, and
municipalities all interpret it differently. This bill draws the line — 500
feet from a school property boundary — and it draws it the same way for every
city in Colorado.

[1:15] **Two: it removes a five-year crash-history requirement before a city
can put a speed camera on a Safe Route to School.** Think about what that
requirement actually says. It tells a community: wait until enough kids have
been hit. We shouldn't be measuring policy in injuries. This bill lets cities
act before, not after.

[2:00] **Three: the School Streets amendment.** This is modeled on Washington's
SB 5595 and on what Paris and other cities have already proven works. It
gives communities the legal ability to designate the block in front of a
school as a 10-mph, pedestrian-priority street — optionally car-free during
arrival and dismissal. We piloted exactly this at Brown Elementary. It
worked. More kids rode. Drivers slowed down. Nobody was confused. What's
missing is the state-level legal foundation to make it permanent and
replicable.

[2:40] I lead a bike bus because I believe the streets around our schools
should be designed for the kids who use them. Right now, in too many places,
they aren't. HB26-1318 — with the School Street amendment — is the most
practical, evidence-backed step Colorado has in front of it to change that.

The House already said yes. I'm asking the Senate to do the same.

Thank you.
